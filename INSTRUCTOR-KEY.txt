PHISHING
email-02.html — Refund — intended EASY
Strong urgency: 5 p.m. today, with a claimed payment hold. Institutional branding,
account reference, and polished writing add realism. Requests a university login
and bank-account confirmation through carolina-studentservices.com, which is
not sc.edu. Sender also uses that external domain.

email-05.html — Research assistant shortlist — intended MEDIUM
Plausible wages and work, a personalized selection claim, and a 2 p.m. interview
scheduling deadline create opportunity pressure. Sender carolina-careers.org is
external; careers.sc.edu.student-applicant-services.com is controlled under
student-applicant-services.com, not sc.edu. The message funnels a university
login into that workspace.

email-08.html — Group survey summary — intended HARD
A familiar peer identity, course details, and a plausible project deadline add
credibility. The apparent sender is unchanged from the legitimate group email;
this simulates spoofing or a compromised account without asserting which.
The key clue is the hover destination blackboard.sc-edu.com rather than
blackboard.sc.edu, combined with a request to sign in again. Sender familiarity
and urgency alone do not determine whether a message is phishing.

JUNK / UNSOLICITED COMMERCIAL EMAIL
email-03.html — Pizza — intended EASY
Prominent product, price, coupon, and tonight-only deadline make the sales
purpose clear. Commercial sender and matching commercial link destinations.

email-06.html — Apartment outreach — intended MEDIUM
A personal greeting and conversational leasing representative make the message
look like individual correspondence. Limited tour appointments add mild time
pressure. The stated outreach purpose and opt-out disclose a commercial pitch.

email-09.html — Course materials checklist — intended HARD
Useful academic planning advice and a restrained newsletter layout precede a
commercial comparison guide. Early rental rates add mild urgency. The footer
discloses an independent marketplace and sponsored seller listings.

All three junk messages are unsolicited in the supplied Campus context. They
promote products or services without a credential theft pretext. Urgency can
appear in legitimate and promotional mail too. A commercial domain alone is
not evidence of phishing. Do not tell participants to classify solely by tone,
formatting, or time pressure. All message-body links remain inactive simulations.
